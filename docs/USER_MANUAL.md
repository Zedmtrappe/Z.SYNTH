# ZYNTH user manual

## Getting started

1. Launch **ZYNTH**. It opens on the first factory preset, ready to play.
2. Choose your audio output in **Options > Audio/MIDI Settings** (top-left of
   the window). On Linux pick ALSA or JACK.
3. Connected MIDI keyboards are opened automatically. You can also play the
   on-screen keyboard with the mouse, or with the computer keyboard after
   clicking on it (A, W, S, E, D... starting from C).
4. Move any fader and listen. Hover a control for its name, value and purpose.
5. Click the preset display for the **Bank > Folder > Preset** menu, step with
   **<** and **>**, or use **BROWSE** to search.
6. Save your own sound with **SAVE AS**. Your sound and settings are also
   restored automatically the next time you start ZYNTH.

### Working with controls

| Action | Result |
|---|---|
| Drag up/down | Change value (faders follow the mouse; hold **Shift** for fine control) |
| Mouse wheel | Step the value |
| Double-click | Reset to default |
| Right-click | MIDI Learn / clear mapping / reset |
| Click then arrow keys | Step; **Shift** = x10, **Home** = default |
| Click a selector (LCD box) | Choose from a list |

A dimmed control is **inactive** in the current configuration (for example the
FM section while ENGINE is ANALOG). It stays where it is and can still be
adjusted; its tooltip says why it is inactive. The panel never rearranges.

The window can be resized; the panel scales uniformly between half and 1.5x
its 1920 x 1200 design size.

Fader caps are colour-coded by section: blue oscillators, teal wavetable,
amber filter, red envelopes, violet LFOs and matrix, green FM, yellow
arpeggiator and sequencer, pink effects, white voice and output.

**Popup editors** keep the panel uncluttered. They float over the panel and
close with their **x** button or **Esc**. Drag a window by any empty part of
its plate to move it (it stays inside the panel); double-click its title bar
to put it back in its usual place:

| Button | Where | Opens |
|---|---|---|
| MOD MATRIX | LFOs title bar | the 8-slot modulation matrix |
| OPERATORS | FM Engine title bar | ratio, fine, level, ADSR, velocity and key scaling of all six operators, with envelope graphs |
| STEPS | Arp & Sequencer title bar | the 16 step values and their gate / accent / slide |
| ADVANCED | Valve window (Output) | valve type, bias, sag, warmth and mix |
| ADV | Delay module | delay presets, wow, flutter, age, spread, ducking |
| ADVANCED | Reverb module | reverb presets, decay, low cut, diffusion, modulation, early/late, width |

## QWERTY keyboard (musical typing)

**QWERTY** (top right of the header) opens a floating window showing a Mac
keyboard. While it is open, the computer keyboard plays ZYNTH (the layout
Logic Pro uses), whether the floating window or the main panel has focus:

| Keys | |
|---|---|
| A S D F G H J K L ; ' | White notes, C upwards |
| W E, T Y U, O P | Black notes |
| Z / X | Octave down / up |
| C / V | Velocity down / up (steps of 16) |
| Tab | Sustain pedal (while held) |
| 1 / 2 | Pitch bend down / up (while held) |
| 3 to 8 | Modulation wheel off, 20 %, 40 %, 60 %, 80 %, 100 % |

Pressed keys light on the drawing and on the panel keyboard; you can also
click the drawn keys. Shortcuts with Cmd, Ctrl or Option are left alone.
Closing the window (or the QWERTY button) releases any held notes.

## Audition

The strip above the keyboard plays a MIDI clip into the current sound, so you
can audition a patch hands-free and tweak faders while it plays.

| Control | |
|---|---|
| Clip display | Click to choose a built-in clip, or *Load MIDI file...* |
| PLAY / STOP | Starts the clip from the top, or stops it (held notes are released) |
| LOOP | Repeats the clip until stopped |
| LOAD MIDI... | Opens any standard MIDI file (.mid) |
| OCT − / OCT + | Plays the clip up to three octaves lower or higher (playback only; the display between them shows the shift) |

The clip plays on the MIDI receive channel, merged with your own playing. All
tracks and channels of a file are merged into one part; whether it plays
polyphonically or monophonically (with glide/legato) is decided by the patch's
VOICE MODE. Notes, sustain pedal, mod wheel, expression and pitch bend are
played; tempo comes from the file. PANIC also stops the player.

Built-in clips (original phrases, generated by `tools/make_audition_clips.py`):

| Kind | Clips |
|---|---|
| Bass | Mono Bassline, Acid Line (slides and accents), Wobble Bass, Synthwave Bass, Techno Rumble, Reese Bass, Disco Octaves, Funk Bass |
| Leads | Mono Lead (legato), Trance Lead, Hoover Riff (long slides), Synthwave Lead, Pentatonic Melody, Jump (contributed) |
| Chords and keys | Poly Chords, E.Piano Ballad (sustain pedal), Gated Chords, Rave Stabs, Jazz ii-V-I, House Chords, 80s Pop, Big Chords (pedal), Brass Stabs, Bells and Keys |
| Pads and textures | Pad Swell, Ambient Drone, Held Chords (for wave sequences), Choir, String Section, Film Swell |
| Arpeggios and plucks | Arpeggio, Pluck Pattern, Harp Arpeggio, Slow Arpeggio, Chiptune |

Loaded files are trimmed to the music: playback starts at the bar holding the
first note and loops at the end of the bar holding the last one, so silent
intros and tails in exported files are skipped.

## Oscillators

Three oscillators with **ON**, **WAVE** (sine, triangle, saw, square, pulse,
wavetable, SuperSaw, HyperSaw and the ROM waves), **RANGE** (32' to 2'),
**SEMI**, **FINE** (cents), **PW** (pulse width, Pulse wave only) and **LEVEL**.

* **SuperSaw** (JP-8000 style) and **HyperSaw** (Virus style) — stacks of 7 or
  9 detuned saws. When one is selected, PW and RING give way to **DETUNE**
  (spread of the saws) and **MIX** (side saws against the centre one). The
  matrix, LFOs and ENV 3 can modulate DETUNE.
* **ROM waves** (ROM Ahh Choir ... ROM Synth Pad) — multi-frame waves voiced
  after 90s workstation ROMs. Each plays its own "sample": an attack, then a
  slowly moving loop, restarting with every note (higher notes run through
  the attack faster). Layer two or three, detune them with FINE, and use the
  Roland TVF / Korg Digital / Alesis QS filters and digital chorus voicings
  for the classic workstation sound.

* **FM 3>1, FM 1>2, FM 1>3** — audio-rate frequency modulation.
* **SYNC** (osc 2 and 3) — hard sync to oscillator 1. Sweep the synced
  oscillator's pitch (e.g. with ENV 3 or the mod wheel) for classic sync leads.
* **RING** (osc 2 and 3) — blends into ring modulation with oscillator 1.
* **ENGINE** — ANALOG (oscillators), FM (operators only), HYBRID (both).
* **SUB** level and **SUB WAVE** (square −1/−2 octaves, sine, 25 % pulse),
  locked to oscillator 1.
* **NOISE** level and **COLOUR** (white/pink).
* **DRIFT** — slow independent pitch wander per oscillator.
* **DRIVE** — mixer overdrive: warmth at low settings, dense saturation high.

### Wavetable

Oscillators set to **Wavetable** play the selected **TABLE**: Analog Morph,
Harmonic Sweep, Vocal Formant, PWM Scan, Digital Bell, Sync Sweep,
Wavefolder, Drawbar Organ, Seq Harmonics, Seq Digital, Seq Vocal, Seq Bells
and Spectral. **POSITION** scans through the table (modulate it from LFOs,
envelopes or the matrix for movement). **SPREAD** offsets oscillator 2 and 3
positions. The four **Seq** tables hold 32 *different* waves, made for wave
sequencing (Seq Harmonics: each wave is the fundamental plus one harmonic, so
a sequence plays the harmonic series as a melody).

### Wave sequence and vector (SEQ / VECTOR ▸)

The button under the wavetable opens a window with two Wavestation-style tools.

* **WAVE SEQUENCE** — from every note-on, the wavetable steps through **STEPS**
  waves (2-32, spread evenly through the table from POSITION). **MODE**: Off,
  Loop, Ping-Pong, One Shot. **TIME** sets each step's length, or switch
  **SYNC** on and choose a **DIVISION** of the tempo. **XFADE** 0 = hard,
  rhythmic switching; 1 = continuous blending (pads). Hold a chord on
  *Wavestation - Rhythmic Seq* to hear it.
* **VECTOR** — switch **ON**, then drag the joystick (or the X / Y sliders)
  to balance four sources: **A** osc 1 (left), **B** osc 2 (top), **C** osc 3
  (right), **D** the FM part (bottom; HYBRID engine). Double-click centres
  it. For vector *movement*, route an LFO, ENV 3 or the matrix to
  **Vector X** / **Vector Y** (as in *Wavestation - Vector Choir*).

## Filter

* **MODEL** — Ladder (warm 4-pole transistor ladder), OTA (punchy cascade),
  SEM (smooth state-variable multimode), K35 (aggressive, screaming), and the
  digital classics: **JP-8000** (resonance thins the bass, gritty whistling
  top, self-oscillates), **Virus** (saturated input, fat low end at high
  resonance, self-oscillates), **Roland TVF** (JD-800 / JV: clean and
  precise, strong resonance that stops short of oscillation), **Korg
  Digital** (Wavestation / Trinity / Triton: clean, moderate resonance) and
  **Alesis QS** (smooth, slightly dark, no resonant peak).
* **TYPE** LP/BP/HP/Notch, **SLOPE** 12/24 dB (K35 has a fixed slope).
* **CUTOFF**, **RESONANCE** (Ladder, OTA, K35, JP-8000 and Virus self-oscillate near maximum).
* **ENV AMT** — envelope 2 modulation (up to 8 octaves); **INVERT** flips it.
* **HPF** — gentle high-pass before the main filter (thins basses, cleans pads).
* **DRIVE**, **KEY** (100 % = cutoff follows the keyboard), **VELO**,
  **LFO 1** (direct LFO 1 depth).
* The curve shows the approximate response of the current settings.

## Envelopes

1 AMP (amplifier), 2 FILTER (filter and any matrix destination), 3 MOD
(direct route via **DEST/AMOUNT**, plus matrix source). All are ADSR with
analogue-style curves: times from 0.5 ms to 20 s. **VELO** sets velocity
sensitivity of the amplifier and of envelope 2.

## LFOs

Two LFOs with **WAVE** (sine, triangle, saw up, ramp down, square, S&H, smooth
random), **TRIGGER** (Free: all voices share one phase; Retrig: restart per
note; One Shot: a single cycle), **DEST** + **AMOUNT** (direct route),
**SYNC** (use **DIVISION** instead of **RATE**), **UNIPOLAR**, **PHASE** and
**DELAY** (fade-in after each note). Both LFOs are also matrix sources.

Tip: for vibrato on the mod wheel set DEST = Pitch, AMOUNT = 0, and route
*Mod Wheel → LFO1 Amount* at about +2 % in the matrix.

## Voice & amp

* **MODE** Poly / Mono (retriggers) / Legato (no retrigger while notes overlap).
* **PRIORITY** (mono): last, lowest or highest note.
* **ALLOCATE** (poly): Rotate (vintage round-robin), Reuse, Lowest.
* **VEL CURVE**, **VOICES** (1–32 physical voices), **GLIDE** + **GLIDE MODE**,
  **BEND** range, **AMP** level, **PAN**.
* **UNISON** with **STACK** (2–8 voices per note), **DETUNE** and **SPREAD**
  (also spreads poly voices across the stereo field).
* **VINTAGE** — per-voice differences in tuning, cutoff, level and position.
* The LED row shows which of the 32 voices are sounding.

## FM operators

Active when ENGINE is FM or HYBRID. **ALGORITHM** chooses how the six
operators connect (the diagram shows modulators above carriers; the loop on
OP6 is its feedback). **DEPTH** scales all modulation, **FDBK** is operator-6
feedback, **LEVEL** is the FM output into the mixer, **VELO** makes playing
harder brighter. **DX MODE** switches the operators to DX7-style maths: the
level faders become logarithmic (99 steps of 0.75 dB, like the DX7's output
levels), operator envelopes move in decibels at a constant rate and stop
exactly on their targets, and the modulation-index and feedback ranges follow
the DX7. The *Classic Machines > Yamaha DX7* presets use it.

Per operator (**OPERATORS ▸**): **RATIO** (0.5, 1, 2 ... 16), **FINE**
(multiplies the ratio by 1 + fine, for inharmonic tones), **LEVEL** (output
for carriers, modulation index for modulators), an ADSR, **VEL** (velocity
sensitivity: soft notes lower this operator by up to 30 dB — on a modulator
that makes soft notes mellower), **K.RATE** (keyboard rate scaling: the
envelope runs up to twice as fast per octave above C4, slower below) and
**K.LVL** (keyboard level scaling: up to -9 dB per octave above C4).

## Modulation matrix

Eight slots of **SOURCE → DESTINATION × AMOUNT** (negative amounts invert).
Sources: LFO 1/2, Env 1/2/3, Velocity, Key, Mod Wheel, Pitch Bend,
Aftertouch, Poly AT, Expression (CC 11), Random (per note), S&H (new value each
sequencer step), Seq Step, Macro. Destinations include pitch (all or per
oscillator), pulse width, levels, FM amount, wavetable position, FM depth and
feedback, cutoff, resonance, drive, HPF, amp, pan, LFO rates and amounts, and
effect amounts (drive, chorus mix, delay mix/feedback, reverb mix).

## Arpeggiator and sequencer

**Arpeggiator**: **ARP ON**, **HOLD** (latch), **MODE** (up, down, up/down,
random, as played, chord), **RATE** (tempo division), **OCTAVES**, **GATE**,
**SWING**. Follows the host's tempo and position when available.

**Step sequencer** (**MODE**):

* **MOD** — the 16 step values become the *Seq Step* matrix source; steps
  with SLIDE glide to the next value.
* **NOTE** — hold a key and the pattern plays monophonically, each step
  transposed ±12 semitones by its value. GATE off = rest, ACC = accent
  (full velocity), SLIDE = glide legato into the next step. NOTE mode
  takes over from the arpeggiator.

**LENGTH**, **RATE**, **GATE** and the shared **SWING** apply. The lit LED
marks the current step.

## Effects

Each module has an **ON** switch and fades in/out smoothly.

| Module | Controls |
|---|---|
| DRIVE | TYPE (overdrive, distortion, fold), DRIVE, TONE, MIX |
| MODULATION | EFFECT selects which modulation effect is shown: Chorus (TYPE: Classic BBD, Roland Digital (JD-800 / JV, bright), Korg Stereo (Wavestation / Triton, wide), Virus (short, with feedback); RATE, DEPTH, MIX), Ensemble (three-phase string ensemble: RATE, DEPTH, MIX), Phaser (RATE, DEPTH, FDBK, MIX) or Flanger (RATE, DEPTH, FDBK, MIX). Each keeps its own ON, so they can be stacked; the EFFECT menu marks the ones that are on |
| DELAY | SYNC, P.PONG, TIME or DIVISION, FDBK, TONE, MIX, TYPE; **ADV ▸** for the rest |
| REVERB | SIZE, DAMP, PRE-DLY, MIX, TYPE; **ADVANCED ▸** for the rest |
| STEREO | WIDTH (0 = mono, 100 % = unchanged, 200 % = wide), CHAR (output warmth) |

### Parametric EQ (output)

The **PARAMETRIC EQ** block (effects row, before SMOOTH and the compressor)
shows the EQ curve with **ON**; click the curve or **ADVANCED ▸** to edit it.
It has five bell bands, each with **FREQ** (20 Hz-20 kHz), **GAIN**
(+/-15 dB) and **Q** (0.3 broad to 10 narrow), a 12 dB/oct **LOW CUT** and
**HIGH CUT** (off at their end stops), and an **OUTPUT** trim. Drag a band's
node on the big curve (left/right = frequency, up/down = gain, mouse wheel = Q,
double-click = flat / filter off) or use the knobs. **With every band at 0 dB,
both cuts off and OUTPUT at 0 dB the EQ is bypassed exactly.** **FLAT**
resets it; **ON** switches it out for A/B. **PRE FX** (on by default) puts it
in front of the delay and reverb, so it shapes the synth itself (and feeds a
PRE FX compressor); switch it off to EQ the whole output instead. It is clean (no colour): the valve,
drive and compressor are there for character.

### Output compressor

**OUTPUT COMP** (right end of the effects row) sits in front of the delay and
reverb by default (**PRE FX** on), so only the dry synth is compressed and
echoes and tails are left alone; switch PRE FX off to make it the last
dynamics stage on the whole mix, before the master section. On the panel: **ON**, **PRE FX**, a gain-reduction VU meter,
**THRESH**, **MIX** (parallel compression), **MAKEUP** (output gain) and
**MODEL**; **COLOUR** in the advanced popup adds the model's analogue
saturation (gentle at 0.5, clearly driven at full); **ADVANCED ▸** opens
the full set. Controls that a model does not have are dimmed.

| Model | Character | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| LA-2A Opto | Optical leveller: smooth, program-dependent two-stage release | THRESH = peak reduction, MAKEUP = gain, RATIO up to 4:1 = COMPRESS / 8:1+ = LIMIT, EMPH (HF emphasis: bright, harsh sounds are compressed harder), COLOUR |
| SSL Bus | VCA bus compressor for glue; very clean | RATIO, ATTACK 0.1-30 ms, RELEASE 0.1-1.2 s or AUTO REL, KNEE, COLOUR |
| 1176 FET | Fast FET limiter | THRESH = input, RATIO 4/8/12/20:1 or All (all buttons: harder, overshooting, grittier), ATTACK 20-800 us, RELEASE 50 ms-1.1 s, COLOUR |
| Fairchild 670 | Variable-mu valve: very soft knee, the ratio rises with level | THRESH, ATTACK 0.2-2 ms, RELEASE 0.3-3 s, COLOUR |

These are ZYNTH's own models voiced after those units, not circuit emulations.

**Faceplates.** The compressor block and its ADVANCED window take on the look
of the selected model: the panel finish, the knobs (with printed 0-10 scales)
and the gain-reduction meter. LA-2A: light grey panel, big black knobs, a
lamp-lit cream VU. SSL Bus: charcoal panel, ribbed knobs with colour-coded caps
(blue time constants, red make-up, yellow mix), a white unlit meter. 1176:
black panel, black knobs on polished skirts, a lamp-lit VU. Fairchild: grey
hammertone, large black skirted knobs, an ivory meter. The controls work the
same on every model.

### Delay types and advanced controls

| Type | Character |
|---|---|
| Digital | Clean repeats; TONE shapes the feedback only (the original ZYNTH delay) |
| Tape RE-201 | Roland Space Echo style: each repeat thinner and darker (head-gap losses), tape saturation, clear wow and flutter, hiss |
| Tape EP-3 | Echoplex style: darker and crunchier than the RE-201, with slow, deep wow |
| Analog DM-2 | Boss DM-2 style bucket-brigade: very dark two-pole roll-off, murky soft saturation, stereo chorus wobble, clock noise |
| Diffuse | Each repeat passes through all-pass diffusers and smears into the next |
| Lo-Fi | Sample-rate and bit reduction with a telephone band |

In the advanced popup: **DELAY PRESETS ▾** loads a starting point for each type
(it sets only the delay and switches it on). **WOW**, **FLUTTER** and **AGE**
scale the type's natural amount of drift, wobble and wear (0.5 = as voiced;
they do nothing on Digital). **SPREAD** lengthens the right-hand delay for
width. **DUCK** turns the echoes down while you play and lets them bloom in
the gaps.

| Type | Character |

| Hall | The original ZYNTH hall |
| Room | Small space with strong early reflections |
| EMT 140 Plate | Dense, bright plate with an instant build-up |

**Reverb EQ.** A parametric EQ on the reverb's wet signal only (the dry sound
is untouched): a 12 dB/oct high-pass (HPF), low shelf, two bells (frequency,
gain, Q), high shelf (+/-15 dB) and a 12 dB/oct low-pass (LPF). The HPF and
LPF are off at their end stops; their square nodes drag left/right. In the advanced popup, drag a band's node on the curve
(left/right = frequency, up/down = gain), use the mouse wheel over a bell to
change its width, double-click a node to flatten it, or use the knobs; FLAT
resets all four. A small live curve sits under the reverb's ADVANCED button.

These are ZYNTH's own algorithms voiced after those units, not emulations of
them. In the advanced popup: **REVERB PRESETS ▾** (two or three classic settings per type),
**DECAY** (trims SIZE's decay time x0.35 to x2.8), **LO CUT**, **DIFF** (echo
density: low = grainier), **MOD** (tail chorusing), **E / L** (early
reflections vs tail) and **WIDTH**.

**Faceplates.** The REVERB block and its ADVANCED window take on the look of
the unit each type is modelled on: finish, fader caps and display colour. For
example: EMT 140 grey-green hammertone, Lexicon 224 / 480L / PCM70 black with
red LED displays and grey fader caps, Bricasti M7 brushed silver with a
blue-white display, AMS blue, Yamaha REV7 light grey with a green display,
Eventide navy with an amber display, Quantec beige. Hall, Room and Shimmer
keep the ZYNTH look.

## Output

**MACRO** is a performance control available as a matrix source (one knob can
move several things). **LIMITER** keeps peaks below 0 dBFS. The small display
shows CPU load, voices, peak level and limiter gain reduction (or, in red, the
output guard's count of automatic recoveries from invalid audio).

**SMOOTH** — its own block in the effects row, between the parametric EQ and
the compressor: a fixed 2.2 kHz dynamic compressor (4:1), with an **ON** switch. It turns the presence
band down only when it gets loud, so harsh notes and
resonances are tamed while the rest of the sound is untouched. **AMOUNT**
lowers its threshold (0 = off, exactly bypassed); the LED ladder beside it
shows the gain reduction (0.5 to 12 dB).

**VALVE** — a valve stage after the effects, seen through a window in the
panel. **ON** switches it in, **DRIVE** sets how hard the valve is pushed:
up to about 80 % it only warms and gently rounds peaks (well under 2 %
distortion even on loud material), and audible distortion starts at about
90 %. The level stays steady and the heaters
glow brighter as you turn it up. **ADVANCED ▸** opens the rest:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| VALVE TYPE | Each has its own curve, harmonics and tone. **12AX7**: high-gain preamp triode; 2nd harmonic leads (sweet, singing), tight bass and a little presence; the first to fuzz. **12AU7**: low-gain triode; the softest, most gradual curve, warm low mids and a rounded top. **EL84**: push-pull pentodes; 3rd and 5th harmonics (gritty "chime"), forward upper mids, leaner bass, compresses early. **6L6**: big power pair; the most clean headroom, deep lows, scooped mids and sparkle, solid compression when pushed |
| BIAS | Cold = cleaner and more symmetric; hot = richer, more even harmonics and grit. On push-pull types it unbalances the pair |
| SAG | Power-supply droop: the valve compresses and blooms on loud notes |
| WARMTH | Output transformer: rounds off the top end and adds low-end weight |
| MIX | Dry/valve blend | The meter
shows both channels with peak hold.

**TRANSFORMER** — an output transformer straight after the valve (its own
strip under the valve window). **ON** switches it in, **TYPE** chooses the
transformer, **AMOUNT** sets the level into it (50 % = nominal studio level,
+12 dBu for a -6 dBFS signal, up to +18 dB hotter; the output level is
compensated). Like the real thing it colours mainly the **low end**: its
distortion rises steeply as frequency falls and level rises (mostly the 3rd
harmonic, which thickens and then "tightens" the bass), while the midrange
and top stay clean; each type also has its own low-frequency corner and
top-end softening. The models follow each transformer's published
specifications and documented behaviour (core material, LF / HF response,
distortion at low frequencies and high level):

| Type | Character | 40 Hz THD at 50 % / 100 % | Low corner | 20 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neve LO1166 | Marinair steel core of the 1073: thick, weighty lows, slightly soft top, a little 2nd harmonic | 0.35 % / 40 % | 8 Hz | -0.8 dB |
| API 2503 | Punchy and wide, the cleanest steel core | 0.16 % / 33 % | 5 Hz | -0.2 dB |
| UTC A-24 | Tube-era line transformer: warm, rounded top, the first to saturate | 0.5 % / 42 % | 18 Hz | -2 dB |
| Haufe V76 | German broadcast: rich lows, the most 2nd harmonic | 0.3 % / 37 % | 14 Hz | -1.2 dB |
| Jensen JT-11 | Nickel core: clean, open and extended, barely saturates | 0.02 % / 1.7 % | 1 Hz | -0.1 dB |

Real transformers are subtle at line level, so the normal mode is deliberately
so: it mostly shapes the deep bass and the top end. **+HOT** models the
transformer *and the amplifier that drives it* pushed far past nominal
(AMOUNT = 0 to +20 dB, level-compensated so the knob changes the colour, not
the volume). The drive stage saturates the whole range and the core then
clamps and compresses the bass. Each type has its own amplifier character:
Neve warm class-A with some 2nd harmonic, API clean until its op-amp clips
hard, UTC and Haufe tube-soft and colouring early, Jensen clean until the top
of the knob. On a 440 Hz note at -9 dBFS that is about 0.5-3.5 % distortion at
25 %, 1.5-8 % at 50 % and 22-28 % at full (the Jensen stays clean until about
75 %). The voicing is exaggerated too: Neve a low bump at 80 Hz and warm low
mids, API a tighter 120 Hz bump and forward 3 kHz, UTC a big 100 Hz bump and
a dark top, Haufe deep 60 Hz weight and full low mids, Jensen still clean.

## MIDI

Note on/off with velocity, pitch bend, mod wheel (CC 1), expression (CC 11),
sustain (CC 64), channel and polyphonic aftertouch, all-sound-off (CC 120),
reset controllers (CC 121), all-notes-off (CC 123).

**MIDI Learn**: right-click a control, choose *MIDI Learn*, move a knob on
your controller. Right-click again to clear. CC 1, 11, 64 and 120+ are
reserved. Mappings are saved with the session.

## Presets

Presets are organised in banks and folders:

* **ZYNTH Originals** — the original library, by category (Bass, Lead, Pad...).
* **Classic Machines** — one folder per famous instrument (Moog Minimoog,
  Sequential Prophet-5, Yamaha DX7, Roland Juno, TB-303, PPG Wave...), each with
  signature sounds in its style, e.g. *DX7 - E.Piano*, *Prophet-5 - Sync Lead*.
  The 90s digital and virtual-analogue machines have their own folders:
  Roland JP-8000, Access Virus, Korg Wavestation, Roland JD-800, Roland
  JV-1080, Korg Trinity-Triton and Alesis QS.
* **User** — your own sounds.

**Favourites** — star any preset: *Add "..." to favourites* at the bottom of
the preset menu, right-click a preset in the browser, the browser's
FAVOURITE button, or click its star. Favourites appear in the **★ Favourites**
folder at the top of the preset menu and in the browser's folder list. When
you pick a preset from a Favourites folder, the **< >** buttons step through
your favourites only (the display shows *FAV 2 / 5*); picking from any other
folder returns them to the whole library. A ★ before the category on the
display marks a favourite. Favourites are kept between sessions. A new
install starts with four favourites (OB-Xa - Jump, AXEL F, JP-8000 - Resonant
Sweep and Synthex - Galactic Lead); un-star any of them and it stays un-starred.

Factory presets are read-only; save a copy with SAVE AS. In the browser:
type to search, filter by category, star favourites, single-click to audition,
double-click to load and close, RENAME and DELETE (with confirmation) for user
presets. User presets are plain files (see `PRESET_FORMAT.md`) and can be
copied between computers.

## Troubleshooting

* **No sound** — check the AUDIO light. If it is dark, no audio device is
  running: open Options > Audio/MIDI Settings. Check MASTER, and that MIDI CH
  is Omni or matches your keyboard.
* **Keyboard does nothing** — open Options > Audio/MIDI Settings and enable
  your MIDI input (it is enabled automatically when present at start-up).
* **Clicks or dropouts** — increase the audio buffer size, or set QUALITY to
  Eco 1x; the OUTPUT display shows CPU load.
* **Stuck notes** — press PANIC.
